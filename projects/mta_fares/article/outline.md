## Lead

Not the arithmetic. The lead is the retirement: in January 2026 the MTA raised the base
fare to $3.00 and, in the same board action, retired the 7-Day, 30-Day and Express Bus
Plus unlimited MetroCards, saying they would "be replaced with the automatic fare cap
for all riders." The cap is weekly. The 7-day pass got a successor. The 30-day pass
didn't.

## The size of the affected population

One ride in thirteen — 7.4% of subway ridership in a sixteen-day September 2024 window —
was taken on a 30-Day Unlimited. Today that product shows 31 rides in the equivalent
window and the 7-Day category is gone from the data entirely. Table: the two windows
side by side.

## What it costs

$35/week × 52 ÷ 12 = $151.67 a month for a rider who caps every week. ETA's proposed
46-ride cap would be $138. The gap is $13.67 a month, $164 a year, on the riders who
ride most.

Before/after, sourced (MTA doc 118601): the 30-Day was $132. A daily rider now pays up
to $151.67 — $19.67 more a month (+14.9%) on a 3.4% base-fare increase. A restored
46-ride cap would cost the MTA roughly $18–27M a year (Q5), under 1% of subway fares.
