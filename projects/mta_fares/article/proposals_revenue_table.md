## The four proposals at a glance

| | What changes | Revenue effect of the change alone | With a 5¢ fare rise | Who comes out ahead | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Monthly cap** | Rides free after $138 (46 rides) in 30 days, on top of today's $35 weekly cap | **−$12M to −$20M** | **+$25M to +$33M** (5¢ on subway + bus ≈ +$45M after rider response) | 5–11% of full-fare riders, the most frequent; up to $13.67/month for someone who caps every week | Moderate: two independent methods agree |
| **2. Inverse distance** | $3.75 under 2 mi · $3.15 for 2–5 · $2.50 for 5–9 · $1.90 over 9 | **~$0 by design**; −$15M after rider response; **−$73M to −$145M** if short-trip riders switch to walking or biking | Not needed (designed to be neutral) | Trips from lower-income neighbourhoods ~6% cheaper on average, but 63% of all trips pay more, including 48% of trips from the lowest-income areas | Good for direction; revenue depends on how price-sensitive short trips are |
| **3. Prepaid reserve** (E-ZPass style) | Riders keep ~a month of fares on account (median ~$49; daily riders ~$150) | **+$10M** interest ($5–18M); unused-balance "breakage" unknown; card-fee savings **+$8M to +$43M** vs weekly batching (up to $46–252M if every tap is billed today) | Not modelled | The MTA; riders lose the interest and must front the money | Low: breakage and current card fees are undisclosed |
| **4. Golden stations** | 5 stations a day, drawn at random from 287 lower-income stations, ride free that day | **−$22M** (excludes riders travelling to the golden station) | **+$15M** (5¢ on subway ≈ +$37.5M after rider response) | Whoever boards at a golden station that day (each eligible station ~6 days a year); a 5-day commuter from an eligible station saves ~$14/yr but pays ~$26 more, **net ≈ −$12** | Moderate for cost; the behavioural pitch (prospect theory) is untested |

**Reading the table**

- Revenue effects are per year, relative to today's fares. Negative = the MTA collects less.
- Scope differs: 1 covers full-fare subway and bus riders; 2 and 4 are subway only
  (~$3.23B); 3 covers full-fare riders' payments. The 5¢ rise is applied to the same
  scope as each proposal (subway + bus ~$3.85B for 1; subway only for 4).
- Proposals 1, 3 and 4 use a modelled rider population; the MTA does not publish
  trips per rider. Treat every range as a range.
- For comparison: the MTA's 2025 subway and bus fare revenue (NYC Transit + MTA Bus +
  SIR) was $3.85B; fare evasion is worth roughly $1B a year in fares not paid.
